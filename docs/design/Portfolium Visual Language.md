## 1. Visual Thesis

Portfolium makes capital feel structured, traceable, and calm.

Its visual identity is defined by five characteristics:

1. **Capital has gravity.** Important financial objects occupy more space and receive stronger typography.
2. **Relationships are visible.** Values are connected to their causes, constituents, periods, and consequences.
3. **The interface recedes.** Containers, controls, and decoration never compete with financial meaning.
4. **Uncertainty remains visible.** Observed, delayed, estimated, projected, and unknown values never look identical.
5. **Density has rhythm.** Concentrated financial inspection alternates with quiet interpretation.

Portfolium should remain recognizable in monochrome, without its logo, and without branded illustrations.

## 2. The Recognizable Portfolium Form

Every Portfolium composition uses three recurring forms.

### 2.1 The Capital Spine

A strong shared alignment connects:

```text
Financial object
Primary amount
Change
Explanation
Evidence
Action
```

Example:

```text
Core Portfolio
€10,913.96
−€55.55 · −0.51% today
Most of today’s decline came from QBTS.
Inspect today’s movement →
```

The spine may be vertical on mobile or extended horizontally through a table.

It must never be interrupted by decorative icons, floating badges, or unrelated controls.

### 2.2 The Exposure Fingerprint

Composition is represented as one complete proportional band:

```text
|──── Technology ────|──── Unknown ────| Comms | Other |
```

The Fingerprint is Portfolium’s signature representation of ownership structure.

It may represent:

- Sectors
- Themes
- Countries
- Currencies
- Asset types
- Accounts
- Goal funding
- Income sources
- Risk contributors

It always represents one complete whole. It never becomes a decorative progress bar.

### 2.3 The Financial Sentence

Material values are followed by a concise interpretation:

```text
€6,093.34 · 55.8%

Your three largest holdings control more than half of the portfolio.
```

The sentence is part of the visual identity. It creates a recurring rhythm:

```text
Number
Meaning
Evidence
```

Portfolium does not leave important numbers visually isolated.

## 3. The Canvas

### 3.1 The canvas is continuous

Portfolium screens are composed as one continuous financial surface.

Sections are separated primarily through:

- Alignment changes
- Whitespace
- Typographic scale
- Meaningful dividers
- Changes in information density

The default canvas is not a grid of independent cards.

### 3.2 The canvas has a financial horizon

Every primary screen establishes, near its beginning:

- The active object
- Its current state
- Its relevant time
- Its confidence or data condition

This creates the horizon against which all detail is read.

### 3.3 Outer space protects concentration

Content width stops growing once relationships become comfortably readable.

Additional viewport width becomes calm outer space rather than wider tables or longer lines.

### 3.4 Alignment is structural

A screen uses a limited number of strong axes:

- Object identity axis
- Primary-value axis
- Numeric-comparison axis
- Explanatory-text axis
- Action axis

Elements that share meaning share alignment.

Arbitrary centering is avoided. Financial information is predominantly left-aligned; comparable numbers are end-aligned.

## 4. Spatial Language

Portfolium spacing expresses conceptual distance.

### Relationship space

Used between attributes of the same financial object.

```text
€4,339.43
39.8% of portfolio value
```

This is the tightest spacing.

### Interpretation space

Used between a result and its meaning.

```text
−€55.55 today

Most of the decline came from QBTS.
```

### Inspection space

Used between repeated records such as holdings, transactions, and events.

It creates enough separation for individual recognition while preserving comparison.

### Disclosure space

Used before methodology, provenance, assumptions, or expanded details.

It indicates that the principal answer is complete and verification follows.

### Question space

Used between different financial questions.

```text
Performance

Holdings
```

This is the largest internal separation.

### Silence

Large empty regions may intentionally remain when no competing information deserves the space.

Portfolium does not fill silence with tips, illustrations, summaries, or promotional widgets.

## 5. Proportion and Scale

### 5.1 Area follows consequence

The visual area assigned to an object is determined by:

- Capital represented
- Materiality
- Decision relevance
- Immediacy
- Explanatory importance

It is not determined by component type.

### 5.2 One dominant scale

Every view has one largest typographic or visual object.

Nothing else may approach its scale unless the view explicitly compares two peers.

### 5.3 Supporting values form families

Related secondary values share:

- Baseline
- Scale
- Formatting
- Alignment

They do not become separate miniature heroes.

### 5.4 Asymmetry expresses judgment

Portfolium prefers meaningful asymmetry over balanced but false equality.

A two-thirds/one-third composition communicates:

```text
Evidence / Interpretation
State / Cause
Structure / Attention
```

Symmetry is reserved for true peers.

## 6. Surface Language

### 6.1 Default surface

The default surface is open canvas.

Financial information does not require a card to exist.

### 6.2 Bounded surface

A bounded surface is permitted when it represents:

- A separate interaction mode
- Editable content
- A selected object
- A warning requiring containment
- A temporary overlay
- A distinct source or account boundary

### 6.3 Inset surface

Inset surfaces hold subordinate information such as:

- Methodology
- Provenance
- Assumptions
- Expanded transaction arithmetic
- Data limitations

They must remain visually subordinate to the claim they qualify.

### 6.4 Elevated surface

Elevation is reserved for temporary layers:

- Menus
- Dialogs
- Inspection panels
- Mobile sheets

Persistent financial content does not float above the canvas.

### 6.5 Surface prohibition

Portfolium never places a bordered card inside another bordered card.

Parent-child relationships are expressed by indentation, alignment, spacing, or a single divider.

---

## 7. Geometry

### 7.1 Geometry is restrained

Portfolium uses quiet, consistent geometry. Rounded forms exist to clarify interaction or containment, not to make every object friendly.

### 7.2 Financial content prefers straight structure

Tables, fingerprints, trajectories, reconciliation lines, and proportional bands use firm linear geometry.

This creates a visual association with:

- Accounting
- Continuity
- Structure
- Ownership
- Traceability

### 7.3 Curves indicate continuity

Curves are appropriate for:

- Time-series trajectories
- Transitional movement
- Carefully controlled data interpolation

Curves are not used for decorative blobs, ornamental backgrounds, or abstract financial symbolism.

### 7.4 Circular forms are exceptional

Circular forms are reserved for:

- Compact object identity
- Explicitly cyclical processes
- A small status marker

They are not used for:

- Allocation
- Goal progress
- Risk scores
- Completion
- General metrics

### 7.5 Corners express purpose

- Open financial sections require no corner treatment.
- Controls have restrained corners.
- Temporary layers may use softer corners to distinguish them from the permanent canvas.
- Warnings do not become more rounded to appear approachable.

## 8. Typography

### 8.1 Typographic character

Portfolium typography is:

- Neutral but not anonymous
- Precise without appearing mechanical
- Dense without becoming compressed
- Calm at large scales
- Highly legible in tabular data

The primary typeface must support:

- Tabular numerals
- Clear currency symbols
- Distinct positive and negative signs
- Broad language coverage
- Legible small metadata
- Stable rendering across platforms

A custom Portfolium cut may eventually replace the reference family, but the typographic grammar remains unchanged.

### 8.2 Typographic roles

#### Financial Hero

Used once per view.

Characteristics:

- Large
- Medium weight
- Compact line height
- Tabular numerals
- No ornamental styling

#### Major Result

Used for material outcomes beneath the Hero:

- Total return
- Capital exposed
- Goal gap
- Transaction total

#### Object Identity

Used for:

- Portfolio names
- Asset symbols
- Goal names
- Institutions
- Benchmarks

#### Financial Sentence

Used for explanatory conclusions. It is visually quieter than the number but stronger than metadata.

#### Constituent Value

Used in holdings, transactions, exposures, and evidence lists.

#### Metadata

Used for:

- Time
- Source
- Method
- Confidence
- Market state
- Lifecycle state

Metadata is quiet, never illegible.

### 8.3 Weight philosophy

Portfolium uses weight sparingly.

- Medium weight establishes financial importance.
- Semibold establishes identity or totals.
- Regular weight explains.
- Heavy and black weights are not part of the product language.

### 8.4 Case

- Sentence case is the default.
- Asset symbols retain their official case.
- Established abbreviations retain uppercase.
- Uppercase section headings are prohibited.
- Decorative letter spacing is prohibited.

### 8.5 Line length

Explanatory financial sentences remain short enough to be read as one thought.

Long methodology and research text uses a narrower reading measure than financial tables.

## 9. The Numerical Voice

### 9.1 Numbers remain stable

All financial numerals use tabular alignment.

A changing number must not cause neighboring information to shift.

### 9.2 Money speaks first

When consequence is being described:

```text
−€70.49 · −6.02% today
```

When structure is being described:

```text
10.7% · €1,171.03
```

### 9.3 Signs are explicit

Every change includes a visible sign.

Static values do not.

### 9.4 Precision communicates confidence

- Exact reconciled money uses currency precision.
- Allocation uses only decision-relevant precision.
- Projections use deliberately reduced precision.
- Approximation is stated in language.
- Unknown precision is never filled with zeros.

### 9.5 Zero is meaningful

Portfolium distinguishes:

```text
€0.00        Confirmed zero
—            Not applicable
Unavailable  Expected but missing
Pending      Not yet final
```

These states may never be substituted for one another.

### 9.6 Negative signs

Portfolium uses a true typographic minus sign, not a hyphen.

The sign remains attached to its value.

### 9.7 Currency

Currency is shown whenever ambiguity is possible.

Mixed-currency views distinguish:

- Native amount
- Converted amount
- Conversion rate
- Valuation time

Converted values never visually impersonate source values.

## 10. Color Language

Color supports meaning after hierarchy has already been established.

### 10.1 Portfolium Rose

Portfolium Rose is the identity accent.

It is used for:

- Active product context
- Selected navigation
- Primary focus
- The most important intentional action
- Brand moments

It is not used for:

- Generic decoration
- Every chart series
- Positive returns
- Risk
- Large background fields

Rose should feel rare enough to remain identifiable.

### 10.2 Capital neutrals

The majority of Portfolium is composed from financial neutrals:

- Canvas
- Primary text
- Secondary text
- Metadata
- Structural divider
- Inset surface
- Elevated surface

Neutral contrast creates the product’s calmness.

### 10.3 Gain and loss

Gain and loss colors are semantic reinforcements.

They always appear with:

- Explicit sign
- Financial wording
- Sufficient non-color contrast

A positive market movement is not automatically a positive user outcome. Color follows the financial meaning of the displayed object.

### 10.4 Attention

Attention uses a restrained warm semantic color.

It indicates:

- A condition worth examining
- Partial data
- Elevated exposure
- An approaching financial event

It does not imply immediate danger.

### 10.5 Critical state

Critical color is reserved for:

- Irreversible destructive actions
- Failed financial reconciliation
- Material source failure
- Conditions requiring immediate intervention

Ordinary losses are not critical-interface states.

### 10.6 Informational state

Cool semantic color may identify:

- External comparison
- Market reference
- Informational source
- Secondary benchmark

It never competes with owned capital.

### 10.7 Unknown state

Unknown and unclassified values use a distinct neutral treatment, not reduced opacity.

Unknown information must remain visible.

### 10.8 Projected state

Projected information uses:

- Lower visual solidity
- Explicit wording
- A distinct line or pattern treatment
- Visible assumptions

Projection is never represented as faded observed truth alone.

## 11. Light and Dark Environments

Light and dark presentations express the same hierarchy.

Dark mode is not a separate visual identity.

### Light environment

- Feels analytical and open
- Uses restrained surface differences
- Avoids large grey card fields
- Maintains strong financial text contrast

### Dark environment

- Feels focused, not theatrical
- Avoids pure black against pure white
- Prevents semantic colors from glowing
- Uses boundaries only where structure requires them
- Keeps charts quieter than primary values

### Parity rule

A design is not complete until:

- Hierarchy survives both environments
- Unknown remains distinct
- Projected remains distinct
- Gain and loss remain accessible
- Borders do not disappear or dominate
- Brand Rose retains the same rarity

## 12. Iconography

### 12.1 Icons are semantic instruments

Icons are permitted only for:

- Navigation
- Actions
- Object identity when the icon is inherent
- Status requiring rapid recognition
- Disclosure direction
- File or source type

### 12.2 Icons never decorate headings

Forbidden:

```text
[chart icon] Performance
[dollar icon] Portfolio value
[shield icon] Risk
```

The words already establish meaning.

### 12.3 Icon style

Portfolium icons are:

- Simple
- Geometric
- Consistent in optical weight
- Predominantly outlined
- Recognizable at small scale
- Free of internal decoration

### 12.4 Icon containers

Icons do not automatically receive colored rounded-square backgrounds.

A container is used only when:

- It identifies an external institution
- It represents an asset logo
- It creates a required touch target
- It distinguishes a temporary status

### 12.5 Asset logos

Asset logos support recognition but never replace:

- Symbol
- Name
- Asset type where ambiguity exists

Missing logos use a stable typographic fallback, not random generated imagery.

## 13. Dividers and Reconciliation Lines

Portfolium uses two distinct line types.

### Structural divider

Separates records or regions within one financial question.

It is quiet and continuous.

### Reconciliation line

Separates constituents from a calculated total.

```text
Unrealized
Realized
Dividends
Fees
────────────────
Total return
```

It carries stronger semantic meaning than a standard divider.

### Rules

- Dividers do not box content.
- Reconciliation lines appear only when arithmetic closes.
- Dashed lines indicate reference, estimate, or projection—not decoration.
- Vertical dividers are used only to compare or distinguish neighboring financial arguments.

## 14. Data Visualization

### 14.1 A chart answers one question

Before drawing a chart, Portfolium defines whether the question concerns:

- Time
- Composition
- Contribution
- Distribution
- Comparison
- Scenario
- Correlation

A chart never combines these merely to appear comprehensive.

### 14.2 Time uses trajectory

Time-series financial values use lines.

The primary owned object is visually strongest.

Invested capital is the primary reference. Benchmarks are secondary.

### 14.3 Composition uses the Fingerprint

A proportional whole uses the Exposure Fingerprint and ranked rows.

Donuts and pie charts are not part of the primary product language.

### 14.4 Contribution uses signed direction

Contribution is shown through:

- Ordered monetary rows
- Diverging bars when many contributors must be compared
- Explicit reconciled totals

Contribution does not use percentage movement as a substitute.

### 14.5 Comparison uses shared baselines

Comparable series share:

- Period
- Scale
- Currency treatment
- Calculation method

If these conditions cannot be met, the comparison is separated and explained.

### 14.6 Scenario uses conditional paths

Scenarios are visually distinct from observed history.

They begin at the current observed state and disclose:

- Assumption
- Range
- Horizon
- Method
- Confidence

### 14.7 Grid

Grid lines establish scale only.

- Few horizontal guides
- No default vertical grid
- No spreadsheet cage
- No decorative zero line
- No alternating plot background

### 14.8 Axes

- Axes are quiet.
- Labels use meaningful temporal anchors.
- Values use compact units.
- Exact values belong in inspection.
- Axis manipulation must not exaggerate movement.

### 14.9 Tooltips

A Portfolium tooltip is a compact financial statement:

```text
18 June 2026

Portfolio value       €10,842.31
Invested capital       €9,799.08
Gain                 +€1,043.23 · +10.65%
```

It never consists only of colored dots and raw values.

### 14.10 Legends

Direct labeling is preferred.

Legends are used only when direct labels would obscure data.

### 14.11 Empty charts

Portfolium does not draw fabricated flat lines or zero-filled plots.

An empty chart explains which financial history is missing.

## 15. Tables and Financial Ledgers

### 15.1 Tables are ledgers

Portfolium tables represent relationships between financial objects. They are not generic data grids.

### 15.2 Identity anchors the row

The first column establishes:

- Symbol or object name
- Full identity
- Relevant relationship

### 15.3 Consequence follows identity

Preferred ordering:

```text
Identity
Current magnitude
Relationship to whole
Immediate consequence
Long-term consequence
Disclosure
```

### 15.4 Numeric alignment

- Comparable values align by endpoint.
- Currency symbols remain visually attached.
- Signs occupy stable positions.
- Percentages align independently from currency values.

### 15.5 Row boundaries

Rows are separated through rhythm and quiet dividers, not individual containers.

### 15.6 Expansion

Expansion reveals provenance, arithmetic, and secondary attributes.

It does not duplicate the row inside a nested card.

### 15.7 Sorting

Sorting follows the primary semantic value of the column.

A column combining money and percentage declares which value controls sorting.

### 15.8 Mobile ledgers

Mobile converts columns into a structured object.

It preserves:

- Identity
- Primary amount
- Relationship
- Immediate consequence
- Long-term consequence

It never preserves the desktop table through horizontal scrolling by default.

## 16. Lists

Portfolium uses three list grammars.

### Ranked list

Used for exposure, contribution, and concentration.

Order communicates importance.

### Chronological ledger

Used for transactions, dividends, and events.

Time controls order.

### Interpretive list

Used for insights, risks, and research conclusions.

Each item follows:

```text
Conclusion
Evidence
Consequence
```

Lists never use decorative bullets when alignment or labels can express structure.

---

## 17. Controls

### 17.1 Controls remain subordinate

Controls use less visual weight than the financial objects they modify.

### 17.2 Primary action

A primary action exists only when the current financial context has one clear next action.

Portfolium does not create a primary button merely because a screen needs visual balance.

### 17.3 Selection controls

Selected state is communicated through:

- Position
- Boundary
- Weight
- Contrast
- Then color

### 17.4 Filters

Filters appear near the evidence they modify.

They do not occupy the Hero region unless period or portfolio scope is fundamental to the Hero.

### 17.5 Menus

Menus use plain language and stable grouping.

Icons appear only when they increase recognition.

### 17.6 Destructive actions

Destructive actions are visually quiet until invoked.

Their confirmation describes the financial consequence, not merely the database operation.

### 17.7 Disabled controls

A disabled control explains why it is unavailable when that reason is not obvious.

Reduced opacity alone is insufficient.

---

## 18. Forms

Financial forms are written as financial operations.

### Structure

```text
Intent
Financial inputs
Resulting calculation
Downstream consequences
Confirmation
```

### Labels

Labels use financial language:

```text
Amount invested
Execution price
Fees paid
Transaction date
```

Not implementation language:

```text
Value
Type
Metadata
Date field
```

### Derived values

Calculated results appear after their inputs and update without spectacle.

### Review

Before committing a consequential change, Portfolium shows:

- What changes
- Which objects are affected
- Whether history will be recalculated
- Whether the action is reversible

## 19. Status and Confidence

Portfolium uses a visible confidence vocabulary.

### Observed

Directly sourced or reconciled.

Normal visual solidity.

### Delayed

Valid but not current.

Timestamp and delay explanation are mandatory.

### Estimated

Calculated from incomplete direct evidence.

Estimate wording remains attached to the value.

### Inferred

A conclusion derived from multiple observed facts.

Evidence must be available.

### Projected

A future conditional result.

Assumptions and horizon are mandatory.

### Unknown

Expected information is unavailable.

Unknown remains visible and materially represented.

### Disputed

Sources disagree or reconciliation failed.

Conflicting values and provenance must be inspectable.

These states are semantic identities, not merely color variants.

---

## 20. Empty States

An empty state explains the absent financial object.

It contains:

1. What does not yet exist
2. Why that matters
3. What creates it
4. One appropriate action, if available

Portfolium empty states do not contain:

- Decorative illustrations
- Jokes about emptiness
- Confetti
- Generic productivity language
- Multiple competing actions

An empty optional section may disappear entirely when its absence requires no action.

---

## 21. Loading States

Loading preserves known financial truth.

### First load

The structure reserves:

- Object identity
- Hero hierarchy
- Dense inspection regions
- Major comparison axes

It does not imitate every icon or row decoration.

### Refresh

Existing values remain visible with their known timestamp.

### Partial load

Independent sections become available when their financial objects are valid.

The page does not wait for unrelated secondary data before showing the Hero.

### Long calculation

The interface names the operation:

```text
Calculating return attribution…
Reconciling transaction history…
Evaluating exposure…
```

Generic “Loading…” is insufficient when the operation has financial meaning.

---

## 22. Error Language

Errors preserve the financial context.

Every error states:

1. What could not be established
2. Which values are affected
3. What remains valid
4. When valid data was last available
5. What the user can do

Portfolium distinguishes:

- Source failure
- Calculation failure
- Partial valuation
- Reconciliation failure
- Permission failure
- Unsupported object

Errors do not replace unaffected financial content.

---

## 23. Motion

Motion communicates continuity and state.

### Entry

New financial content settles into its stable location.

### Exit

Removed content releases quickly.

### Number changes

Changed values transition once. They do not count from zero, roll, pulse, or flash repeatedly.

### Reordering

Sorted records move only when motion helps preserve their identity.

### Expansion

Disclosure reveals the relationship between summary and detail.

### Charts

Period changes preserve recognizable trajectories and baselines where mathematically valid.

### Live updates

Only the affected value or object acknowledges the update.

### Emotional target

Motion should make Portfolium feel:

- Stable
- Precise
- Continuous
- Deliberate

Never:

- Playful
- Excitable
- Urgent by default
- Game-like

---

## 24. Responsive Expression

Portfolium does not have desktop and mobile identities. It has one financial grammar expressed at different widths.

### Desktop

Supports simultaneous comparison.

### Tablet

Supports grouped interpretation.

### Mobile

Supports serial reasoning.

### Responsive invariants

The following never change:

- Primary financial question
- Hero
- Object identity
- Money-before-percentage rule
- Time and confidence
- Constituent traceability
- Narrative order

### Responsive variables

The following may change:

- Simultaneous versus sequential layout
- Table versus structured object
- Direct label versus ranked list
- Contextual panel versus full-width sheet
- Amount of visible secondary evidence

---

## 25. Visual Tempo

Every substantial experience follows:

```text
Orient
Declare
Inspect
Interpret
Resolve
```

### Orient

Light density. Establish object, scope, time, and confidence.

### Declare

Light density. Present the primary financial truth.

### Inspect

High density. Compare constituents, events, or evidence.

### Interpret

Medium or light density. Explain consequence.

### Resolve

Light density. Offer disclosure or action.

Two dense regions never touch without an interpretive reset.

A reset may be:

- A financial sentence
- A total
- A conclusion
- Question space
- A clear change of alignment

---

## 26. Brand Imagery

Product interfaces rely on financial objects rather than decorative imagery.

Illustration is permitted only when it:

- Explains a new financial concept
- Represents an unavailable real-world object
- Supports onboarding outside active financial work

Illustration must not appear beside:

- Losses
- Risk
- Errors
- Empty holdings
- Missing data
- Goal shortfalls

Photography is reserved for editorial or institutional contexts, not product decoration.

---

## 27. Reports, Exports, and Shared Views

Portfolium’s visual language survives outside the application.

Every exported or shared financial view preserves:

- Object identity
- Scope
- Currency
- Valuation time
- Confidence
- Financial sentence
- Exposure Fingerprint where relevant
- Source and methodology disclosure

Exports do not become generic branded documents.

The logo may identify the source, but the financial grammar identifies Portfolium.

---

## 28. Visual Quality Test

A proposed design passes only if all answers are yes.

### Identity

Can the user identify the financial object before reading its metrics?

### Hierarchy

Is there exactly one dominant visual answer?

### Consequence

Does visual weight correspond to financial importance?

### Traceability

Can aggregates be followed to their constituents?

### Time

Does every changing value expose its period or timestamp?

### Confidence

Can observed, delayed, estimated, projected, and unknown states be distinguished without color?

### Rhythm

Does the experience alternate declaration, inspection, and interpretation?

### Restraint

Would removing a border, icon, card, color, or label make the meaning clearer?

### Mobile

Does narrow-screen presentation preserve reasoning rather than geometry?

### Recognition

If the logo and Portfolium Rose disappeared, would the Capital Spine, Financial Sentences, Exposure Fingerprint, numerical voice, and visual tempo still make the product identifiable?

If any answer is no, the design is not ready.
