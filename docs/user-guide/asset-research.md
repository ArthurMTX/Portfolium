# Asset Research

Look up any stock, ETF, or crypto asset and see the full evidence behind it — market fundamentals, your personal position, and your own investment reasoning, all in one page.

## Overview

Asset Research is Portfolium's dedicated deep-dive view for a single asset. Unlike the Dashboard or Charts, which focus on your portfolio as a whole, Asset Research answers one question at a time about one company or fund: what is it, how has it performed, what does it cost, and — if you own it — how has it actually affected you.

The page is deliberately split into two kinds of information:

- **Neutral market evidence** — fundamentals, valuation, and risk data that don't change based on whether you own the asset
- **Personal position evidence** — your holdings, transaction history, and your own investment notes for that asset

Your personal notes never alter the neutral research shown to you; they're recorded and displayed separately.

## Finding an Asset

1. Open **Asset Research** from the main navigation
2. Type a company name or ticker symbol into the search bar
3. Choose a result from the dropdown, or press Enter to open the top match

If you haven't searched yet, the page shows your **recent searches** so you can quickly return to assets you've looked at before. You can clear this history at any time.

!!! tip "Market movers"
    The search page also surfaces **Trending**, **Gainers**, and **Losers** tabs showing notable market activity, so you can discover assets even without a specific search in mind.

## The Asset Research Page

Once you open an asset, you'll see its name, ticker, ISIN (if available), asset type, country, and trading currency at the top, along with the current price and today's movement.

Below that, a **My relationship** banner immediately tells you where you stand with this asset:

- **You own it** — shows your current position value
- **You have historical activity** — you've bought and sold it before, but don't currently hold it
- **You don't currently own it** — no transactions recorded

From here you can **add the asset to your watchlist** or jump straight to **recording a transaction**.

The rest of the page is organized into tabs:

- **Overview**
- **Financials**
- **Valuation**
- **Risk**
- **My Position**

### Overview

The Overview tab answers "what is this asset?" It combines:

- A business description (when available), plus founding year, employee count, and headquarters
- Key snapshot figures: market capitalization, revenue growth, net margin, P/E ratio, analyst price target, and implied upside
- A **price chart** covering 1 month, 3 months, year-to-date, 1 year, or the full history
- **Theme and sector classification** — if you own the asset, Portfolium shows the sector and industry it belongs to, along with any investment **themes and subthemes** it has been classified under (each with a confidence score)

!!! note "Price chart markers"
    If you've transacted in this asset, the price chart plots your **buy, sell, transfer, conversion, and split** events directly on the timeline, along with your average cost line, so you can see exactly when you traded relative to the price.

### Financials, Valuation, and Risk

These three tabs present the same categories of fundamental data used throughout Portfolium's [Detailed Metrics](../detailed-metrics/index.md) — profitability and growth, balance sheet strength, market scale, valuation multiples, analyst estimates, and volatility/risk indicators. Rather than repeating the full explanation of each metric here, Asset Research shows the current values framed as questions ("Is the business growing profitably?", "What does the market expect?", "How unstable has the asset been?").

For definitions of individual metrics like P/E ratio, EPS, ROE, beta, or volatility, see the [Detailed Metrics](../detailed-metrics/index.md) reference.

If a metric can't be evaluated for a given asset, Portfolium shows this clearly rather than guessing or hiding it silently — you'll see a note that the evidence isn't available instead of a blank or fabricated value.

### My Position

If you own or have owned the asset, the **My Position** tab shows the personal side of the story:

- **Current holdings** — market value, quantity, portfolio weight, and unrealized profit/loss
- **Return equation** — how your lifetime P&L breaks down into unrealized gains, realized gains, and dividends received
- **Cost and break-even** — your average cost, current price, cost basis, market value, and (if you're underwater) the gain needed to break even
- **Realized history** — quantity sold, average sell price, proceeds, and realized P&L, if you've closed out any part of the position
- **Portfolio consequence** — how much of your portfolio's value and volatility this position contributes, and its estimated effect on your portfolio today
- **Transaction history** — every recorded buy, sell, dividend, split, transfer, and conversion for this asset

If you no longer hold any shares, the tab still preserves your closed position's historical transactions and realized results.

## Investment Notes

Each asset has room for your own personal investment thesis, separate from the neutral market data above it.

Click **Add thesis** (or **Edit thesis** if one already exists) from the My Position tab to record:

- **Why I own it** — your thesis
- **Conviction** — low, medium, or high
- **Risks I accept**
- **What invalidates the thesis**
- **Target price** and/or a free-text target description
- **Time horizon** — short, medium, or long term — and an optional target date

!!! tip "Notes are personal, not predictive"
    Your investment note is a personal record, not a signal Portfolium factors into fundamentals, valuation, or risk scoring. It exists so you can track *why* you made a decision and revisit it later.

You can delete a note entirely from the same editor if your thinking has changed and you'd rather start fresh.
