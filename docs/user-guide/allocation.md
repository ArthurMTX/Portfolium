# Allocation

See exactly where your capital lives, sliced by sector, country, asset class, currency, theme, or company size.

## Overview

The Allocation page answers one question from six different angles: **which parts of your portfolio own your money?**

Instead of a single pie chart, it lets you switch between six different "lenses" on the same capital, so you can spot concentration risk that a single view might hide. A portfolio that looks well-diversified by sector might still be heavily concentrated in one country or one currency.

The page always reflects your currently active portfolio.

## Accessing Allocation

1. Log in to Portfolium
2. Click **Allocation** in the main navigation
3. The page loads using your active portfolio's positions

## The Six Lenses

Switch between views using the tabs at the top of the page:

| Lens | What it groups by |
|------|--------------------|
| **Sector** | Business sector of each holding (e.g. Technology, Healthcare, Financial Services) |
| **Country** | Country of the company or issuer behind each holding |
| **Asset class** | Type of asset (stocks, ETFs, crypto, and so on) |
| **Currency** | The currency each holding is denominated in |
| **Theme** | Thematic classification, such as AI, clean energy, or semiconductors |
| **Market cap** | Company size bucket — Mega Cap, Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Micro Cap, plus separate buckets for Funds/ETFs and Crypto assets |

Each lens ranks its categories from largest to smallest by portfolio value, and shows the exact value and percentage share next to each one.

!!! note "Theme allocation"
    Theme allocation is built on Portfolium's asset theme classification, which tags individual holdings with one or more investment themes. Because a stock can belong to more than one theme, theme percentages don't have to add up to the same totals as the other lenses.

## Drilling Into a Category

Click any category in the ranking to expand it. The panel next to the ranking updates to show:

- The total value and percentage that category represents
- How many individual positions make up that share
- A list of those positions, each with its own value and weight, so you can jump straight to an asset's research page

This lets you go from "42% of my portfolio is in Technology" to "and here are the specific holdings responsible for that" in a single click.

## Concentration Snapshot

At the top of the page, a metrics strip gives you an instant read on concentration:

- **Top holding** — the largest single position as a percentage of your portfolio
- **Top 3** — combined weight of your three largest positions
- **Top 10** — combined weight of your ten largest positions
- **Largest country** and **largest sector** — your single biggest exposure in each of those dimensions

!!! tip "Why concentration matters"
    A portfolio where the top 3 holdings represent a large share of total value carries more single-stock risk than one spread across many smaller positions, even if both are "diversified" by sector.

## Unclassified Exposure

Not every holding can always be classified — some assets may be missing sector, country, or theme data. The page surfaces this directly in an "Unknown exposure" section, showing how much of your capital currently falls outside a clean classification for a given lens, and links you to review and fix it from the [Assets](assets.md) page.

## Diversification Summary

A short narrative section summarizes your spread in plain language, for example how many countries, sectors, and companies or funds your capital touches, and calls out your largest single concentration by sector and by country.
